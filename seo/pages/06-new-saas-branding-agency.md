**Intro**

SaaS branding is harder than consumer branding for one reason: the brand lives inside the product. A beverage brand shows up on a can. A SaaS brand shows up in an empty state, a changelog, a 404 page, a Stripe receipt and a technical doc read by someone who hates marketing.

That's why every identity we ship comes with design tokens and a design.md. Your engineers get the brand as variables. The marketing site and the product stop drifting apart.

We've done this for [CodeRabbit](/work/coderabbit), [Databuddy](/work/databuddy), [Sparkles](/work/sparkles), [Conare](/work/conare) and [Switchfrog](/work/switchfrog). Dev tools, analytics, AI infrastructure, agent detection. The common thread is a technical buyer who can smell a stock template from the hero section.

**H2: What's different about branding a SaaS company**

- **The buyer is technical.** Developers and technical founders distrust polish that reads as marketing. The brand has to feel native to the people using it. For CodeRabbit that meant an identity that felt like it belonged in a terminal, not on a billboard.
- **The surface area is enormous.** Logo, yes. But also icon sets, syntax highlighting themes, OG images, email templates, a docs theme. We scope the system for where it actually has to live.
- **The product changes every two weeks.** A brand that only works for today's feature set is dead by Q3. We build systems, not one-off logos, so the brand holds its shape while the product keeps moving.
- **Category is unsettled.** Half our SaaS clients are inventing their category name as they go. Positioning comes before identity, or the identity has nothing to stand on.
