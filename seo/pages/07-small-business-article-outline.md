# OPTIONAL `/branding-agency-for-small-business`

**Target:** branding agency for small business (590/mo, difficulty 0).

## The problem

Difficulty zero and 590 searches a month is the easiest win on the list. But the searcher is a plumber, a café, a two-person consultancy. Your floor is $10,000. A service page here brings traffic that bounces.

## The option that works

Write it as an honest article, not a service page. Title it around the decision the searcher is actually making, and route them to the two things you sell that fit a small-business budget: the $1,200 teardown and the $2,500 strategy sprint.

- Title: `Do You Need a Branding Agency for Your Small Business? | Happy Pizza Studio`
- H1: `Do you need a branding agency for your small business?`
- Angle: most don't, yet. Here's how to tell, and what to buy instead at each stage.

## Outline

1. **The honest answer.** Under roughly $50k revenue, no. Buy a good template and spend the money on customers. (Pick the threshold you actually believe.)
2. **The three signals you've outgrown DIY.** You're losing deals to a worse competitor who looks better. You've hired and the new people are embarrassed by the deck. You're about to raise or expand and the brand will be audited by strangers.
3. **What a branding agency charges, and why.** Your real prices. Explain what $10k buys versus $500 on a marketplace. Don't trash the marketplaces.
4. **The cheaper first step.** The teardown: $1,200, one week, scored against competitors, credited toward a project. This is the conversion point.
5. **If you're a startup rather than a small business.** One paragraph and a link to `/startup-branding-agency`.

## Decision

Build this only if you're comfortable with a page that tells most of its readers not to hire you. It will rank. It will also bring a few hundred visits a month of people you'll mostly turn away, with the teardown as the catch.
